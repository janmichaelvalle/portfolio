Product growth case study

Everbreed — Turning Trial Signups Into Activated Customers

Using product analytics, onboarding, and activation measurement to improve the path from trial to paid.

Role
Product Manager
Focus
Growth + Product Analytics
01

The problem

Signups Were Not Becoming Customers

Everbreed was generating a high volume of trial signups, but only about 15% converted to paid subscriptions. Paid advertising increased signup volume, but those additional signups were not translating into meaningful product engagement or paying customers.

The team decided to shift the focus from maximizing signup volume to attracting higher-intent users and helping them experience Everbreed's value during the trial.

02

Acquisition strategy

Shifting From Signups to Quality

Paid acquisition was driving more registrations, but the additional signup volume was not producing corresponding engagement or conversion. The company stopped paid advertising and shifted toward organic acquisition, prioritizing higher-intent users over signup volume.

We also introduced a credit-card requirement before starting a trial. Previously, users could create an Everbreed account without providing payment information.

More signups ≠ More engaged customers

The reduction in trial volume was an intentional shift away from optimizing for signup volume alone.

03

Onboarding

Designing for Activation

Before: New users logged into Everbreed and primarily encountered a hero banner before entering the product.

After: We introduced a guided onboarding flow designed to personalize the experience and move users toward their first meaningful value moment.

04

Feature discovery

Extending Guidance Into the Product

After seeing positive results from the onboarding experience, we expanded contextual education into other areas of Everbreed.

When users visited certain parts of the product for the first time, they received short contextual prompts or tours explaining the feature and helping them understand what to do next.

05

Measurement framework

Defining Activation

Conversion told us whether someone became a paying customer, but it did not tell us whether they were meaningfully using Everbreed. I created an activation framework using behavioral data from core product workflows.

Activated = at least 3 actions across at least 2 of 3 core behaviors
Rabbit management3+ breeder/rabbit records
Pedigree activity3+ pedigree edits
Scheduling activity3+ schedule events

06

Supporting analysis

Using SQL to Measure Behavior

I queried Everbreed's underlying product and subscription data to classify users based on product activity and payment status. This allowed us to measure activation at the cohort level rather than relying only on surface-level metrics such as signups or logins.

CASE
  WHEN
      (COALESCE(rb.total_breeders,0) >= 3
       AND COALESCE(p.total_pedigree_edits,0) >= 3)
   OR (COALESCE(rb.total_breeders,0) >= 3
       AND COALESCE(e.total_schedule_events,0) >= 3)
   OR (COALESCE(p.total_pedigree_edits,0) >= 3
       AND COALESCE(e.total_schedule_events,0) >= 3)
  THEN 'Activated'
  ELSE 'Not Activated'
END AS is_activated

Impact

Fewer Signups. More Paying Customers.

Fewer signups. More paying customers.

Following the acquisition, trial-gating, and onboarding changes, trial-to-paid conversion increased from approximately 15% to 58%. Although average trial volume decreased from ~103 to ~40 users per cohort, the average number of paying customers increased from ~15 to ~23.

Several changes happened together: paid acquisition was stopped in favor of organic acquisition, a credit card became required to begin a trial, the onboarding experience was redesigned, and contextual product education was later expanded.
